Stripe UTC vs local time at month end: how to date payouts in QuickBooks

Stripe reports can use UTC or your account time zone, so month-end sales and payouts can land in the "wrong" month in QuickBooks. Here's how to pick one time zone, date the journal, and check charges in transit.

Published 14 November 2026

Short answer: Stripe's financial reports can be viewed in your Stripe account's time zone or in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). That choice changes both which rows fall inside a date range and how dates are shown. If your QuickBooks books use a local time zone and the Stripe export is in UTC, a charge or payout near midnight on the last day of the month can land in the "wrong" month. Pick one time zone, use it for the Stripe report and for the journal date, and leave anything not yet paid out in Stripe Clearing so the clearing balance equals Stripe's ending balance for that same date and zone.

Why the month-end numbers disagree

A Stripe payout is a net figure: charges, minus fees, minus refunds and disputes, plus or minus adjustments. Even when that arithmetic is right, the dates on the export may not be the dates your books use.

Stripe's own docs on report configuration say you can view financial reports in your account time zone or in UTC, and that a day's data runs from 12:00 am through 11:59 pm in the time zone you selected. The payout reconciliation report groups automatic payouts by estimated arrival date, not by the date your bank posts the deposit, and the report data can arrive after the money does.

Three different dates show up in Stripe exports, and they answer different questions:

DateWhat it meansUse it for
Charge or refund created (or the report date of the balance transaction)When the payment activity happened, in the report's time zoneWhen revenue, refunds or fees hit the Profit and Loss, if you book by activity date
available_onWhen those funds become available in your Stripe balanceUnderstanding pending vs available; not usually the bank date
Payout arrival date (estimated arrival on the payout reconciliation report)When Stripe expects the payout to arrive at your bankDating the transfer from Stripe Clearing to the bank, then matching the bank-feed line (whose posted date may be a day later)

If you mix these — for example, dating sales on UTC created dates and dating the payout on the bank's local posting date — month-end will almost always be off, even when every amount is correct.

A worked example (illustrative times only)

Example: a charge is created at 23:30 UTC on 31 October 2026. The clocks have already changed in the EU (the last Sunday of October), so Central European Time is UTC+1. In the US, Eastern Daylight Time is still in effect until the first Sunday of November, so it is UTC−4.

Time zoneLocal date and timeMonth it falls in
UTC31 October 2026, 23:30October
US Eastern Daylight Time (UTC−4)31 October 2026, 19:30October
Central European Time (UTC+1)1 November 2026, 00:30November

A European seller who downloads an October Balance report in UTC will include this charge. The same seller, with books in CET, would put it in November. Neither is "wrong"; they are two different cut-offs. The error is using both in the same close.

The same split happens on the payout side. A payout with an estimated arrival of 1 November 00:15 UTC may post to a US bank on 31 October local time, or the other way around. Match the bank-feed line by amount and payout id, not by assuming the dates are identical.

How to set a cut-off policy and stick to it

  1. Write down one time zone for Stripe reporting. In the Stripe Dashboard, open a financial report (Balance or Payout reconciliation) and select either your account time zone or UTC. Use the same choice every month.
  2. Download the report for the month in that zone. Stripe treats the selected dates as inclusive, from 12:00 am on the start date through 11:59 pm on the end date in that zone. For automatic payouts, the itemized payout reconciliation CSV lists every charge, refund, dispute, fee and other balance transaction in each payout. If you use manual payouts, or instant payouts, Stripe says the payout reconciliation report may not apply in the same way — use the Balance report instead. Stripe also notes that it cannot identify which transactions are inside an instant payout, so you reconcile those against your transaction history yourself.
  3. Date the settlement journal on the payout's arrival date (the date the report uses for that payout), and put the payout id in the journal number or memo. The journal should contain only the balance transactions that belong to that payout. Revenue can still follow the charge date if that is your policy; the settlement entry is what has to match the bank.
  4. Do not force the clearing account to zero at month end. Money that is still pending or available in Stripe, or a payout that has not yet arrived at the bank, should remain in Stripe Clearing. That leftover is a timing balance, not an error.
  5. Run the month-end check: Stripe Clearing in QuickBooks, on the last day of the month in your chosen time zone, should equal Stripe's ending balance (available plus pending) for the same date and zone. If you also have funds held in reserve, keep those on a separate reserve account — see how to record a Stripe reserve or held funds in QuickBooks — and include them in the balance-sheet check.
  6. If the numbers still differ, look for a payout in transit (arrived in Stripe's report in one month and in the bank feed in the next), a refund or dispute booked on a created date that sits in a different month from the payout, or a bank rule that added the deposit as income. The last of those is covered in Stripe deposits double counted in QuickBooks.

This is a reconciliation method, not accounting or tax advice. If a period is already reconciled or filed, check with your accountant before you change dates.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing a UTC report with a local-time Profit and Loss. Change one of them so both use the same zone, then compare again.
  • Dating the journal on the bank posting date while building it from a report grouped by estimated arrival date. The amounts should still match; the months may not. Use the report's arrival date on the journal and match the later (or earlier) bank line by amount.
  • Booking "charges in transit" as a plug to income. If the charge has already been recorded, the gap is on the balance sheet (Stripe Clearing or undeposited funds), not on the Profit and Loss.
  • Using instant or manual payouts with a payout reconciliation workflow. Switch to the Balance report and reconcile like a bank account, instead of expecting a batch for each payout.

Other reasons a payout does not equal sales (fees, refunds, disputes) are in why your Stripe payout doesn't match your bank deposit. To set up the clearing account, see how to set up a Stripe clearing account in QuickBooks Online.

FAQ

Should I use UTC or my local time zone for Stripe in QuickBooks?

Either can work. What matters is using the same time zone for the Stripe report and for the dates in QuickBooks, every month. Many businesses pick the time zone their books already use.

Which Stripe date should I put on the QuickBooks journal?

Date the settlement journal (the one that moves money from Stripe Clearing to the bank) on the payout's arrival date from the report you used. The bank-feed date can differ by a day. Charge created dates are for when you recognise the sale, if you book by activity date.

Why doesn't my Stripe Clearing account go to zero at month end?

It often should not. Charges that are still pending or available in Stripe, and payouts that have not yet arrived at the bank, belong in Stripe Clearing. The account should equal Stripe's ending balance for the same date and time zone, not necessarily zero.

Do instant payouts follow the same cut-off?

Not through the payout reconciliation report. Stripe says it cannot identify which transactions are included in an instant payout, so you reconcile those against your transaction history, often with the Balance report, using the same time zone policy.
