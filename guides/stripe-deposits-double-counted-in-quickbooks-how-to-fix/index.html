Stripe deposits double counted in QuickBooks Online: why it happens and how to fix it

Stripe income doubled in QuickBooks Online? The sale is already recorded and the payout was added again as income. Undo the added deposit, then match it or post it as a transfer from Stripe Clearing.

Published 9 November 2026

Short answer: Stripe income gets counted twice when a sale is already recorded in QuickBooks Online (by an invoice payment, a sales receipt, an app or connector, or a journal entry through a Stripe clearing account) and the Stripe payout that later lands in your bank feed is added as new income too. A payout is not new income: it is money moving from Stripe to your bank. Undo the added deposit in Bank transactions, then either match it to the record you already have or post it to Stripe Clearing so it becomes a transfer. Then pick one source of revenue and stick to it.

How to tell your Stripe income is double counted

  • Income on the Profit and Loss report for a month is far above the gross charges Stripe shows for the same dates, often close to sales plus payouts.
  • In the bank account register, Stripe payouts are posted to an income account (such as Sales) while the same sales are also recorded somewhere else.
  • Your Stripe Clearing account (if you use one) keeps growing month after month instead of staying close to your Stripe balance.

Compare like with like: use the same date range and time zone in Stripe and QuickBooks. Stripe's payout reconciliation report shows which charges, fees and refunds make up each payout.

QuickBooks' own help article also lists a sale recorded as both an invoice and a sales receipt as a cause of doubled income. Keep the one with the payment attached.

Steps to fix it

  1. Pick one month and note Stripe's gross charges, fees, refunds and payouts for it.
  2. Find the added payouts: go to Bank transactions and open the Categorized tab (newer screens call it Posted). Look for Stripe payouts that went to an income account.
  3. Undo each one. The transaction goes back to the For review (or Pending) tab. Nothing is deleted from your bank feed.
  4. Post it the right way: match it to the record you already have (use Find match or Find other match), or, if you book Stripe through journal entries, categorise it to Stripe Clearing.
  5. Re-run the Profit and Loss for the month. Income should now tie to Stripe's gross charges (and refunds, depending on how you book them). If you use a clearing account, its balance should be close to your Stripe balance.
  6. Stop it recurring: turn off auto-add on any bank rule that catches Stripe deposits, so they wait in For review, and use one source of revenue per sale.

Example: one month with 1,000.00 of Stripe charges, 32.00 of fees (made-up figure) and one payout of 968.00. The sales and fees are booked by journal entry through Stripe Clearing, and the bank feed line for the payout was added to Sales by mistake.

Common mistakes when fixing it

  • Deleting the journal entry instead of the extra deposit. That removes the fee and refund detail. Keep the record that has the detail, and fix the bank line.
  • Excluding the bank line when nothing else records the payout. If no other transaction moves that money into the bank account, the bank account in QuickBooks won't reconcile. Exclude only real duplicates.
  • Matching a net payout to a gross invoice payment. The amounts differ by the Stripe fee, so QuickBooks won't suggest the match. Add the fee as a negative line on the bank deposit first.
  • Changing transactions in a period that is already reconciled or filed. Talk to your accountant first.

Other reasons the payout and your sales differ (fees, refunds, timing) are in why your Stripe payout doesn't match your bank deposit. To set up the clearing account itself, see how to set up a Stripe clearing account in QuickBooks Online.

FAQ

Why is my Stripe income doubled in QuickBooks?

Usually because the sale is already recorded (invoice payment, sales receipt, connector or journal entry) and the Stripe payout in the bank feed was added again as income. Undo the added deposit and match it or post it as a transfer.

Should a Stripe payout be categorised as income?

Not if the sale is already recorded elsewhere. A payout moves money from Stripe to your bank, so it belongs against the account that holds the Stripe money (for example Stripe Clearing) or should be matched to the existing deposit.

Can I just exclude the Stripe deposits in the bank feed?

Only if another transaction already records that same money arriving in the bank account. Otherwise your bank account in QuickBooks will not reconcile.

What if the month is already reconciled?

Undoing reconciled transactions changes balances you have already signed off. Check with your accountant before you change a reconciled or filed period.
