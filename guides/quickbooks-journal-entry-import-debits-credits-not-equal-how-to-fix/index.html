QuickBooks journal entry import: debits and credits not equal, and how to fix it

QuickBooks Online rejects your journal entry CSV because debits and credits do not balance? Check each journal number on its own, then fix rounding, signs, cents and shared journal numbers.

Published 9 November 2026

Short answer: QuickBooks Online checks each journal entry in your CSV on its own, grouped by the Journal No. column. The whole file can balance and one journal can still be off. Sum debits and credits per journal number, find the ones that differ, and fix the cause. The usual causes are amounts with more than two decimals, a negative number in the wrong column, a missing second line, two entries sharing one journal number, or amounts in cents. The exact wording of the error varies, but it always comes down to one journal whose debits and credits are not equal.

Find the journal that doesn't balance

  1. Open the CSV in a spreadsheet. Assume Journal No. is in column A, Debits in E and Credits in F.
  2. Add a check column: in G2 enter =ROUND(SUMIF(A:A,A2,E:E)-SUMIF(A:A,A2,F:F),2) and fill it down. Any row that is not 0 belongs to a journal that is out of balance.
  3. Or use a pivot table: rows = Journal No., values = Sum of Debits and Sum of Credits. Compare the two columns.
  4. Look for amounts with more than two decimals: =E2<>ROUND(E2,2) shows TRUE on lines that will cause rounding drift.
  5. Fix the cause (see the table below), save as CSV again and re-import one journal first.

Causes and fixes

CauseWhat it looks likeFix
RoundingFees calculated with a percentage formula (for example 5.075) while the other line uses 5.08 or 5.07Round every amount to two decimals before totalling. With Stripe, use the fee column from the export instead of recalculating it.
Negative amountsA refund entered as −20.00 in the Debits columnUse positive amounts only and put the amount in the opposite column (a credit instead of a negative debit).
Missing second lineA fee debited to Stripe Fees with no matching creditEvery amount needs its other side, here Cr Stripe Clearing for the same amount.
Shared journal numbersTwo days or two payouts use the same Journal No.Give each entry a unique number, such as SC-2026-11-02, and one date per journal.
Amounts in cents10000 on one line and 100.00 on anotherStripe API data uses the smallest currency unit. Divide by 100 for two-decimal currencies. Zero-decimal currencies such as JPY are not divided.
Text in amount cellsCurrency symbols, thousand separators or spacesUse plain numbers such as 1000.00. See the other import errors.

Worked example (illustrative numbers only)

Example: the fee line was calculated with a spreadsheet formula instead of taken from the export. The fee figure is made up for the example.

Journal No.,Journal Date,Account Name,Description,Debits,Credits
SC-2026-11-02,11/02/2026,Stripe Clearing,Gross charges,175.00,
SC-2026-11-02,11/02/2026,Sales,Gross charges,,175.00
SC-2026-11-02,11/02/2026,Stripe Fees,Fees,5.975,
SC-2026-11-02,11/02/2026,Stripe Clearing,Fees,,5.98

On screen both columns may show 180.98, but the debits are really 180.975, so the journal is 0.005 off. Fixed: use the fee from the Stripe export (5.98 here) on both fee lines. Debits 180.98 = credits 180.98.

Stripe-specific causes

  • Refund and dispute rows have negative amounts in the Stripe export. Turn them into positive amounts on the opposite side (Dr Refunds / Cr Stripe Clearing) instead of copying the minus sign. See Stripe refunds and chargebacks: how to book them.
  • Fees booked from a different report than the charges, so the totals come from different date ranges or time zones. Build each journal from one export.
  • Each Stripe balance row should tie out (gross − fee = net). If a row doesn't, the journal built from it won't either. The free payout check shows this per category.

FAQ

Why does QuickBooks say my journal is not balanced when the file totals match?

QuickBooks checks each journal number on its own. One journal can be off by a cent while another is off by the same cent the other way, so the file total still matches.

Can I use negative numbers in a journal entry import?

The safest choice is positive amounts only, with each amount in the Debits or the Credits column. A refund becomes a credit to Stripe Clearing and a debit to Refunds.

How do I find a one-cent difference?

Sum debits and credits per journal number with SUMIF or a pivot table, and check for amounts with more than two decimals. Fees recalculated with a percentage formula are the usual source.

Should I import the whole file at once?

Import one journal first. When that works, import the rest, so a single bad journal does not block a whole month.
