Whatnot fee calculator: your real profit per sale after fees

Work out real profit per Whatnot sale after the 2026 tiered commission (8% US Standard), 2.9% + $0.30 processing, item cost and shipping. Worked examples and a free spreadsheet.

Published 28 September 2026 · Updated 15 November 2026

A card sells for $50 on a Whatnot stream and it feels like a $50 night. It isn't. Commission, payment processing, the cost of the item, shipping supplies and your own label all come out before you see profit. This guide walks through the math by hand so you can price lots with confidence.

Fee figures below are from Whatnot's Seller Fees and Commissions Schedule (page updated 1 November 2026, checked 15 November 2026). Since 21 September 2026 commission is tiered: the rate depends on your category and on your total sales in a 4-week period. Promotions and custom terms vary, so confirm in Seller Hub, which is the source of truth for payouts. Not affiliated with Whatnot.

The key point: two fees, two different bases

This is where most spreadsheets go wrong:

  • Commission is a percentage of the item's final sale price only. It doesn't include buyer-paid shipping or tax.
  • Payment processing is a percentage of the total order value (item + buyer shipping, plus buyer tax where applicable) plus a fixed fee per order.

So when buyer shipping goes up, your processing fee goes up too, but your commission doesn't.

Step 1: Find your commission rate

Whatnot's commission depends on your category and your tier. Everyone starts on Standard; your total sales in a 4-week period set the tier for the next period (US Tier 1 starts at $15,000). US Standard-tier rates:

Canada uses the same rates (C$ thresholds). In the UK, Germany, Austria and the Netherlands Standard is 6.67%, and VAT is charged on fees; France and Belgium charge a flat 4% on Sports and TCG. Japan isn't tiered: 8% plus JCT (4% for Coins). The full tier tables are in the Whatnot fees breakdown.

Step 2: Find your processing fee

US/CA/AU: 2.9% of order value + $0.30 per order. UK/EU: 2.42% + £/€0.25, plus VAT.

Step 3: Do the math for one lot

Example (US, Standard tier, 8% category such as TCG): $50 sale, buyer pays $8.70 shipping, so the order value is $58.70.

  • Commission: 8% × $50 = $4.00
  • Processing: 2.9% × $58.70 + $0.30 = $2.00
  • Total fees: $6.00, so net payout from the sale is $44.00

Now take off your own costs to get profit:

  • Cost of goods (what you paid for the card), e.g. $25
  • Supplies (top loader, team bag, mailer), e.g. $1
  • Any shipping cost you pay above what the buyer paid

Profit = sale − commission − processing − COGS − supplies − your shipping cost Margin = profit ÷ sale

With $25 COGS and $1 supplies, that $50 sale is $18 profit, a 36% margin, not $25.

Step 4: Use the rate for your tier (and any promotion)

Your rate is locked for the whole 4-week period. If you reached US Tier 1 last period, the same $50 Sports or TCG sale costs 7.75% × $50 ≈ $3.88 in commission instead of $4.00. Processing doesn't change with your tier.

Promotional rates (for example new-seller 0% fees or limited-time category promotions) override your tier rate while they run. Whatnot previously ran a reduced-commission promotion on high-value orders; its current fee schedule page doesn't list it, so only count on it if your Seller Hub receipts show it. When in doubt, use the rate shown on the order in Seller Hub.

Step 5: Work out break-even before you list

Start from the margin you want and work backwards. Roughly: minimum price ≈ (COGS + supplies + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − commission % − processing % − target margin). Remember that buyer shipping also adds to processing. Run your cheap lots through this. Low starting bids on low-cost items are where fixed fees quietly eat the margin.

Step 6: Review the whole show

After a stream, put every lot in a sheet with sale, shipping, COGS and supplies, and flag anything under your target margin. A handful of losing lots can cancel out a good night.
